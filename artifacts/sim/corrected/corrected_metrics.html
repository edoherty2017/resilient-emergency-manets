What a SAR planner would ask

Decision-oriented metrics the raw counts hide — how long a distress call takes at the tail, how much of the network is actually up, and where it's chronically dark. Latency shading: green ≤60 s, amber ≤10 min, red beyond.

What the counts hid: "29,000 death events" understated the always-on problem — those modes leave ~46% of solar fleet-time dark (118 chronically dark sites). And "SOS delivered" hid a latency cliff: uniform duty_sync delivers 94.7% of SOS but with a 35-minute p95 (retry loops), while rotate_lb / selective_duty deliver ~99.4% at 33–62 s p95 — with ~90% fleet availability. Every mode still shows rare 1–2 h worst-case incidents (the retry ceiling), so the max column is a shared limitation, not a differentiator. Still no single winner: rotate/selective pay ~3× duty_sync's site-dark time. But the operating envelope is now visible.
